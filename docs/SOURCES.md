# Sources

Where Sri Lankan law lives online, how to reach it, and what blocks us.
Surveyed 2026-10-04. Re-check a source only if its row says something changed.

## Scope

The goal is the law as it stands now. Phases, in order:

| Phase | What | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Constitution** | Supreme law. Numbered by Articles and Chapters, not Sections and Parts | Base 1978 text and consolidations up to the 21st: parliament.lk `/files/pdf/constitution/` (2026-10-11, see parliament.lk table). Amendments 17th–22nd: documents.gov.lk act `0/YYYY` |
| 1 | **Consolidated statutes** | The starting text everything else applies to | 2006 consolidation now (already scraped); 1980 Revised Edition later |
| 1 | **Acts** | New law and amendments since the starting text | documents.gov.lk (2006+, complete in English); parliament.lk acts listing (official PDFs 1950–2026, surveyed 2026-10-10; `etl.fetch parliament` 1956 on, started 2026-10-10) |
| 1 | **Regulations** | Binding law that exists only in gazettes (tax rates, import rules, emergency regulations) | documents.gov.lk extraordinary gazettes, filtered by description |
| 2 | **Judgments, SC decisions on Bills** | Explain the law but don't change its text | supremecourt.lk, courtofappeal.lk, parliament.lk |
| — | Bills, Hansard, provincial statutes | Low value or scattered | Later, if at all |

Model: starting text (a consolidation) plus Acts after it as amendments, checked against the 2024 consolidation.

**Tamil is out of scope (user, 2026-10-06).** All 1174 Tamil `documents` rows (702 MB of PDFs, 978 with raw extraction) were deleted from Neon and their 2150 R2 objects removed; `etl.fetch acts` no longer lists Tamil files. One Tamil file had the same sha256 as a non-Tamil row, so its R2 objects were kept. To bring Tamil back, drop the filter in `discover_acts` and re-run fetch.

**Sinhala files are not kept (user, 2026-10-10).** The 1554 Sinhala `documents` rows stay (listing record in `meta`) but their files are gone: 2620 R2 objects (1551 source PDFs plus their raw/v2 extractions, 987 MB of PDFs) deleted; `sha256`, `r2_key`, `bytes`, `raw_key` and the timestamps are NULL, and `error` = 'out of scope: Sinhala (2026-10-10)'. 3 Sinhala files had the same sha256 as an English row, so those objects were kept. `etl.fetch` only queues `lang = 'ENGLISH'`. Two lankalaw rows labelled English with documents.gov.lk `_S.pdf` URLs (ids 4919, 5242) were kept because the `acts` graph uses them. To bring Sinhala back, drop the lang filter in `fetch()` and re-run.

**Files under the wrong key (checked and fixed 2026-10-11, `etl.docs keys [--fix]`).** Each Act/Law's "may be cited as ... No. N of YYYY" against its key: 35 differ. Rule: the printed name is the listing's title for the key -> key right, printed number a misprint (27 kept: act:13/1958 "1938", act:7/1985 "1935", act:3/1969 "8 of 1969", act:16/1983 "10 of 1983", act:31/1993 "35", act:34/1990 "32", act:30/1991 "16 of 1989", act:9/1991 "79 of 1988"; lankalaw's 1973-76 Laws print "1978", e.g. law:44/1973 Administration of Justice Law). Otherwise, or when the text is identical to the law the printed number is held as, the file is another law: act:28/1969 (parliament.lk 5684.pdf = Fugitive Persons 29/1969) switched to lankalaw's HTML (Anuradhapura Urban Council (Special Provisions)); **dropped, no source has the right file:** act:56/1957 (parliament 3696.pdf = Holidays (Amendment) 57/1957; listed as Income Tax (Amendment)), act:77/1981 (3001.pdf = NRESA 78/1981; listed as Appropriation), act:2/1991, act:3/1991, act:5/1991 (lankalaw's 1991 HTML pages hold the 1995 Acts of those numbers; listed as Sri Lanka Islamic Centre, Sahabdeen Trust Foundation, Ruhunu Kala Kawaya (Incorporation) Acts), act:5/2012 (lankalaw 05-2012_E.pdf and parliament pbills/906.pdf are one file, sha256 d2a1c728, = BOI (Amendment) 3/2012; listed as Central Colleges Past Pupils' Association), act:42/2023 (parliament 6284.pdf = Appropriation (Amendment) 42/2022). Each dropped file's `documents.error` says why. Not checked: documents.gov.lk's Sinhala-only rows for these numbers (77/1981), or other sources for the 7 missing laws. Backups before the fix: `backups/{docs,chunks,links,acts}_20261011_092*.csv.gz`.

**Acts coverage vs documents.gov.lk** (compared by act number + language from both indexes, 2026-10-04): 3,835 files in both. **167 are only on documents.gov.lk**: the 1980 acts (lk starts in 1981) and everything after 2026-04. **144 are only in lk**: files the old site had that the new listing drops. Use both.

**Not in lk_datasets at all:** lankalaw consolidations (2006, 2024, 1980 Revised Edition), the 1978 Constitution base text, SC decisions on Bills, provincial statutes.

**Text quality:** `doc.txt` is just the PDF's text layer, with **no OCR and no font fixing**. Before ~2000 it is empty (scans). Sinhala is still in the broken legacy FM font (`YS% ,xld`). So it saves downloading but not text extraction. The legal_docs branches stopped updating in 2026-04, when documents.gov.lk moved.

| Laws.Africa / Indigo | Open platform, Akoma Ntoso XML, many African countries | **Editors** apply amendments in a tool; each amendment creates a new version of the document |
| Etcheverry, Real, Chavallard (arXiv 2501.16794) | Applying amendments to French law automatically | Small LoRA-tuned model; **about 63% success** on a hard bill |
| Lexis (Shepard's Knowledge Graph), Westlaw (KeyCite) | US case law and statutes | Decades of editorial citation tracking; Harvey licenses Lexis rather than build its own |

Conclusion: nobody gets a reliable amendment graph from AI alone. The big players use deterministic structure, people for the hard cases, and the graph as the product. Fully automatic amendment application is still around 63% in research.

Measured here: of the 742 Acts since 2006, **373 (50%) are amending Acts by title** ("X (Amendment) Act"), so the title alone links each one to the Act it amends.

## Authority

- From 1978, laws are enacted in Sinhala and Tamil with an English translation (Constitution Art. 23). Acts say "the Sinhala text shall prevail", so **Sinhala is authoritative for law from 1978 on**.
- Ordinances from before 1978 were enacted in English, so English is the original there.
- **Checked 2026-10-11 (the user asked: English is what the government gives, and consolidations are private products, so are they enough?):**
  - *Art. 23(1)* (as substituted by the 16th Amendment, read from `constitution:21`): laws are enacted in Sinhala and Tamil "together with a translation thereof in English"; Parliament decides per law which text prevails; for older laws, the text it was enacted in. So English is officially a translation. **Every Act we hold that names a prevailing text names Sinhala:** 1,438 Acts (1980s 14 of 541 held, 1990s 474 of 487, 2000s 431 of 435, 2010s 305 of 308, 2020s 214 of 215); none names English or Tamil.
  - *Art. 24:* the languages of the courts are Sinhala and Tamil; English only under a direction of the Minister of Justice with Cabinet concurrence (24(4)). No such direction was found (web search, 2026-10-11), so "courts work in English" is practice we have not sourced.
  - *Consolidations:* parliament.lk's Constitution consolidations call themselves an **"unofficial edition"** (Bills Office, Parliament), both the up-to-17th and the up-to-21st (2023). lankalaw's "consolidated up to 2006" file names (`1981Y16V526C`) match **LawNet's** (lawnet.gov.lk, Ministry of Justice: `docs/statutes/leg_enact_1981/...`, `stats_1956_2006/...`), so it is probably a copy of the government's own consolidation, not lankalaw's product; LawNet's "Penal Code Consolidated" is the 1980 chapter with later amendments in it ("[§2, 32 of 1991.]", inserted page "281A", loose-leaf style). Not established: under what authority LawNet's consolidations were made, or whether the 1980 edition had official supplements.
  - Working rule until then: the law is the enacted text plus its amending Acts; consolidations (parliament.lk, LawNet, lankalaw) are aids to check our own reading against, not sources of law.
- Law text is not copyrighted (Intellectual Property Act No. 36 of 2003, s.8). Editorial consolidations (lankalaw) are grey: take the law text, not the site's own arrangement.

## documents.gov.lk (Government Printer)

A Next.js app. Every listing is one Server Action (`TableDataAction`) called with a different `apiEndpoint`. `etl/spiders/acts.py` already handles this; for another section, change `PAGE` and the endpoint.
Files: `https://documents.gov.lk/api/content-file-proxy?file=/<uploadedFile>`.

### Extraordinary gazettes (surveyed 2026-10-20, issue #6; listing only, no PDF opened)

Same Server Action as Acts (`TableDataAction`, page `/web/extra_gazettes`). Record: `gazetteNoText` ("2511/09"), `date`, `descriptionEnglish` (the Sinhala/Tamil fields carry the same English text), `contents[]` per language (`extra-gazette-content/<file>.pdf`, file names like "PG 8089 (E).pdf"). Pages of 1,000 work; past a few thousand rows a page fails on RSC `T` rows (long strings split out), so a failed page is re-asked as 4 smaller ones. ### Blockers

- **Large pages:** past a few thousand rows, long strings are split into RSC `T` rows, and the `{"data"` row regex in `acts.search()` fails. Page at 5,000 or fewer, or regex over the raw body.
- **Rebuilds:** the Server Action id changes on every redeploy. `discover()` re-reads it each run; never hard-code it.
- **Paging:** upstream ordering is unstable across pages, so dedupe by `id`.
